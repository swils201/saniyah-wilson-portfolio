## Research
### Spiral Design Process
The spiral design process is an approach to designing a product in loops starting by testing and designing around constrains and risk and looping as the product develops. This reduces wasted resources on potential failure that could be found and resolved in prototyping. Once a prototype is complete, feedback is meant to be collected, improving the product as it is being designed, continuing to reduce waste.

For my final project, I will apply this process in my design for this week. I am choosing to prioritize smaller models which will allow testing of the key functions of the design, with this in mind.

### FDM Anisotropic
FDM printing typically produces parts which are anisotropic or weaker in the Z direction than in the X and Y. This behavior will influence the strength of the part, and potentially its ability to meet its intended purpose, based on the orientation of the part. In my design, I will attempt to prioritize the strength of any of the flexures, fits, or areas of interaction between the parts through orientation in those axis'.

**Resource:**  
- https://rapidmade.com/isotropic-vs-anisotropic-strength-in-3d-printing/

### SLA Shrinkage 
SLA printed parts typically experience shrinkage post-curing, a behavior that is important to consider when designing assembled parts. It mainly affects the dimensioning and allowable clearances/tolerances of each part. From the resource I've used, it seems shrinkage generally for resin can range from 1.5-3 percent. These percentages can be applied to chosen clearance dimensions to accommodate for shrinkage. Additionally, there are existing values aligning with different geometric limitations for different part designs. If I choose to do a resin print, I will pull values from the table provided in the resource I've cited below to accommodate for the post curing behavior of SLA. I will reflect on the values found in previous resin projects regarding the print shrinkage as well to make similar design choices.

**Resources:**  
- https://sgd3d.co.uk/3d-printing/sla-3d-printing-accuracy-tolerances/  
   
### Laser Cut Kerf
In laser cutting, kerf refers to the material which is burnt away by the layer beyond the original design intent. This changes with material and laser settings. Determining the kerf produced by the material and chosen settings for the cut of your part is important so you may offset the laser, or accommodate clearance values to match the intent. If I choose to use laser cutting in my design, I will do a test coupon of the material to determine the setting which limits kerf, and accommodate laser settings as appropriate.

To begin the design process, I started sketching the main parts which I imagine making up the design. The product is made up of seven small parts total. The box which can be filled with dog treats or food. Three panels with different pattern cuts and T-shaped extrudes two sides can be slid into the matching T-Shaped slots in the box. In those pattern cuts, knobs can be inserted and turned to rest and fit. The dog then moves these around and as they do, are exposed to new areas to grab food.
